## Notes

- Ceiling: 200 of 200 runs across the four arms solved (n=50 each). "No task lost" holds, but on this corpus it could not have failed.
- The verdict depends on which plain run it is judged against. The protocol's same-window plain gives "inside the noise" (1.06, 0.98 to 1.16); the baseline's plain gives "pass" (1.03, 0.96 to 1.10). The two plain runs are the same arm, and they differ by 0.97 (0.89 to 1.06, n=50), about 4k raw prompt tokens per task. At n=50 on this corpus the study cannot tell 1.03 from 1.06, and the bar's 1.05 sits inside that spread.
- What the flags did, against the same SHA with flags off (n=50): raw 1.05 (0.97 to 1.14), billed-equivalent 1.01 (0.98 to 1.06), rounds 1.04 (0.97 to 1.13), all inside the noise. On HumanEvalFix, where both flags change the brief, raw 0.97 (0.90 to 1.03) and billed-equivalent 0.98 (0.94 to 1.01), n=26, inside the noise but in the expected direction. The raw ratio is pulled up by polyglot (1.22, 1.03 to 1.52, n=12), where the flags-on brief keeps the edit-and-check pairing and differs from flags-off only in its first line and the Companions section: six of the 12 polyglot tasks took one more round with flags on and scale-generator took 10 rounds against 4 after a wrong first attempt, which is run-to-run behaviour, not the brief.
- Fixed text per round: on task pairs with equal rounds, flags-on read a median 145 fewer prompt tokens per round than flags-off (mean 220, n=27) and wrote a median 140 fewer cache tokens per task. Against plain, the flags-off head reads a median 896 more per round (n=34) and flags-on 749 more (n=33); the baseline measured about 1.0k for 41f9ba6 and d59aa98, so the skill-description cut in both head arms and the lean brief together take off roughly 250 tokens a round. NEXTGEN-5 expected row 4 to remove about 1.3k per call and reach parity on HumanEvalFix; here about 750 tokens a round of atlias text remains (the MCP server's tool definitions, the skill listing and the rest of the brief), and HumanEvalFix sits at 1.02 raw (0.94 to 1.08) and 1.05 billed-equivalent (1.02 to 1.09) against same-window plain (n=26).
- The gate-runs-the-check half never fired: 0 gate-run checks in 50 flags-on runs. On every HumanEvalFix and smoke run that edited (31 of 31; nothing-to-fix made no edit) the model ran a command after its last edit (the prompts name the check), so no stop wanted one; on polyglot and refactor the gate can name no check. Its intended target, the CanItEdit check round, is not in this corpus. This study measures the lean brief, and says nothing about gateRunsCheck beyond "it did not get in the way".
- Holds: one per head arm (n=50 each), both on lint-the-file, both "The reply says a check passed, but no check ran this turn" after the model ran node lint.mjs, which printed lint clean. False by any run, not by atlias's rule: the classifier does not call lint.mjs a check, and with gateRunsCheck on the gate still had nothing to name (package.json has no test script, and lint.mjs is not a check name it knows). Each cost one more round (148.8k and 148.0k raw against 114.6k for plain). The baseline saw the same hold at 41f9ba6 and not at d59aa98; now it is 2 of 2 at the head. Holds that said no check ran when atlias's own rule shows one: 0 in both arms.
- Cancelled hook calls: 0 of 497 (flags off) and 0 of 531 (flags on), none in the transcripts either, so every pair is a clean pair and the clean-pair ratio equals the full one.
- Cost in dollars as Claude Code reports it, flags-on against same-window plain: 1.09 (1.05 to 1.14, n=50), outside the noise; output tokens were 1.21x plain's (59.8k against 49.5k in total), mostly the two-pass summary line, and output is not in the prompt-token ratio.
